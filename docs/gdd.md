## Game concept

Animal VS is a browser game where players choose formidable real-world animals
for virtual one-on-one battles against human or CPU opponents. Animal levels,
combat statistics, and training-facility bonuses determine the outcome through
an automatic simulation.

The appeal is choosing a favorite contender, discovering its strengths, watching
a short showdown, and developing animals and their training facilities across
repeated matches. A daily-turn management layer adds decisions about construction,
training, and animal welfare without limiting battle participation.

The confirmed game name is Animal VS. Animals look imposing rather than cute.
Combat is stylized and non-graphic:
animals charge, swipe, bite, recoil, and retreat without blood, wounds, or death.
The game does not depict or encourage real animal fighting.

### Design status

The following direction is confirmed:

* The game is named Animal VS and targets players aged 13 and older.
* Players select animals and watch automatic battles, without combat inputs.
* Quick Battle finds human opponents and offers CPU fallback after 15 seconds
  without a suitable match; dedicated CPU Battle is also available.
* CPU battles award less XP and fewer resources than human battles.
* Matchmaking considers animal level only, not facility or readiness bonuses.
* Animal levels persist and are earned through matches.
* Players build and upgrade training facilities through facility cards.
* Facilities provide combat bonuses, progression benefits, and future unlocks.
* Base management uses 24 turns per UTC day, with carryover capped at 48 turns.
* Construction and upgrades complete on the next daily reset.
* Welfare maintenance grants optional readiness bonuses; neglect never reduces
  base stats, levels, or health and never prevents an animal from battling.
* Battles do not consume management turns.
* All six starter animals remain available; facilities can unlock future animals
  and other features.
* Visuals use low-poly 3D animals and arenas, with a total art budget under $5,000.
* The game runs in a web browser.
* Weather is randomly generated by the server and shown before animal selection.
* Terrain is randomly generated by the server and revealed after both choices
  are locked, before players accept the battle.
* The first release uses normal level-based matches, without an equal-level mode.

The numerical combat rules, facility costs and effects, CPU reward reduction,
matchmaking ranges, environmental profiles, and release targets below remain
proposals for simulation and playtesting, not validated balance claims.

### Design pillars

* Make every roster animal a credible, formidable contender.
* Make levels and statistics matter more than random variation.
* Explain outcomes through visible statistics and readable battle events.
* Keep battles available even when few human players are online.
* Make daily management choices meaningful without penalizing time away.
* Reward participation without selling combat advantages.

## Audience and platform

The intended audience is players aged 13 and older who enjoy animal matchups,
collectible progression, base development, and short competitive sessions. Confirm
age, privacy, and consent requirements for intended launch regions before a
public release; the target age alone does not establish legal compliance.

The first release supports desktop and mobile browsers with mouse, touch, and
keyboard controls. No installation, browser extension, or controller is required.
Target current stable Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari at release.

The intended session is a handful of matches lasting approximately two to five
minutes. A single battle presentation should usually take 20 to 60 seconds.
These are design targets to measure during prototyping.

## Core gameplay loop

1. Sign in and inspect available management turns, resources, and facilities.
2. Spend turns on construction, upgrades, animal training, or welfare if desired.
3. Choose Quick Battle or CPU Battle and see the server-generated weather.
4. Choose an animal, inspect its level and bonuses, and lock the selection while
   terrain remains hidden.
5. Find a human opponent or accept a clearly labeled CPU opponent.
6. Reveal both animals, their levels, terrain, and resulting modifiers.
7. Accept the matchup and watch the server-resolved animated battle.
8. Read the result, XP, resources earned, and any level gained.
9. Start another battle or return to facility management.

Player agency comes from selection, understanding matchups, and choosing which
animals and facilities to develop. The daily-turn budget governs management
actions, not combat actions. There are no reaction inputs, tactics menus, or
turn-by-turn combat commands in the first release.

## Animal roster

### Eligibility

Select living, real-world species with substantial size, strength, defensive
capability, or natural weapons. Predators are not the only eligible contenders:
large defensive herbivores also belong in the roster.

Exclude pets, deliberately helpless opponents, cute mascot designs, extinct
animals, fantasy creatures, and human fighters from the first release.

Animal profiles can include verified natural-history facts later. Combat
statistics are game abstractions, not scientific measurements or predictions
about real animal encounters.

### Proposed starter roster

All six animals are available at level 1 when an account is created. Players
never need to unlock a competitive contender before entering a battle.

| Animal           | Combat identity       | Visual attack style     |
|------------------|-----------------------|-------------------------|
| Bengal tiger     | Agile attacker        | Pounce and claw swipe   |
| African lion     | Balanced bruiser      | Lunge and bite          |
| Brown bear       | Durable heavy hitter  | Heavy swipe and shove   |
| Hippopotamus     | Slow, resilient tank  | Charge and jaw snap     |
| White rhinoceros | Defensive powerhouse  | Horn feint and charge   |
| Nile crocodile   | Armored ambush hunter | Low lunge and bite      |

Battles take place in varied fictional arenas, such as grassland, rocky ground,
forest, wetland, and snowfield. Weather can include clear skies, rain, heat,
cold, or strong wind. The server randomly chooses and reveals weather before
animal selection; it randomly chooses terrain after both players lock their
selections. Terrain is revealed before either player accepts the matchup.

Movement and habitats are intentionally abstracted so every pairing is playable.
Terrain and weather ratings are game-balance inputs, not scientific claims about
real animals or predictions about real encounters.

### Initial base statistics

Statistics use a nominal 1 to 100 scale at level 1. Derived statistics can exceed
100 as animals level up. Each initial profile has a 240-point base budget.
Equal budgets do not establish equal strength because combat formulas weight
statistics differently.

| Animal           | Vitality | Power | Guard | Speed |
|------------------|----------|-------|-------|-------|
| Bengal tiger     | 60       | 70    | 45    | 65    |
| African lion     | 65       | 65    | 55    | 55    |
| Brown bear       | 80       | 70    | 65    | 25    |
| Hippopotamus     | 85       | 65    | 75    | 15    |
| White rhinoceros | 75       | 60    | 85    | 20    |
| Nile crocodile   | 65       | 75    | 75    | 25    |

These are prototype inputs. Tune them using matchup simulations before claiming
that any animal is balanced.

### Terrain and weather effects

Each animal has a terrain affinity from 0 to 100 for every terrain and a weather
tolerance from 0 to 100 for every weather condition. A score of 50 is neutral.
These ratings affect Power, Guard, and Speed; they do not change Vitality or
maximum health. The combined modifier is capped so environmental conditions
influence matchups without overwhelming species profiles:

```text
environmentMultiplier = clamp(
  1 + (terrainAffinity + weatherTolerance - 100) / 1000,
  0.90,
  1.10
)
effectivePower = floor(trainedPower * environmentMultiplier + 0.5)
effectiveGuard = floor(trainedGuard * environmentMultiplier + 0.5)
effectiveSpeed = floor(trainedSpeed * environmentMultiplier + 0.5)
```

Apply the modifier after level scaling and facility/readiness bonuses, before
battle resolution. Show terrain, weather, facility/readiness bonuses, and each
animal's resulting modifier before acceptance.
The combat formulas use these effective Power, Guard, and Speed values.
The exact affinity and tolerance tables remain prototype values to tune against
the full matchup and condition matrix. Attack animations have no separate
mechanical effect; unique abilities, status effects, and elemental damage are
out of scope.

## Combat model

### Statistics and levels

Each account owns a separate level and experience total for each animal.
Species determines the base profile; players do not allocate stat points.

| Statistic | Mechanical effect                              |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------|
| Vitality  | Increases maximum health                       |
| Power     | Increases damage from a successful attack       |
| Guard     | Reduces incoming damage                        |
| Speed     | Improves hit chance relative to opponent speed  |

The proposed level cap is 20. When a selection locks, the server freezes the
animal's level, completed facilities, welfare readiness, and progression bonuses.
Calculate level-derived statistics first:

```text
levelMultiplier = 1 + 0.03 * (level - 1)
derivedStat = floor(baseStat * levelMultiplier + 0.5)
```

Apply facility and readiness bonuses next, using the rules in
[Training facilities and daily management](#training-facilities-and-daily-management):

```text
trainingMultiplier = 1 + min(0.10, applicableFacilityBonus) + readinessBonus
trainedStat = floor(derivedStat * trainingMultiplier + 0.5)
maximumHealth = 500 + 10 * trainedVitality
```

Calculate the multiplier separately for each statistic. Readiness adds either
0 or 0.03; therefore the maximum pre-environment bonus is 13% per statistic.
Environment modifies trained Power, Guard, and Speed, but not trained Vitality.
Pending construction, later training, and daily resets cannot change an already
locked matchup.

A level 20 animal has a 1.57 multiplier before rounding. Progression improves
strength, but does not guarantee victory against every lower-level opponent.
The first release uses normal level-based matches only. Both players see the
actual level difference before the battle begins; an equal-level mode is deferred
until playtests show whether it is needed.

### Resolution rules

A battle consists of up to 60 rounds. Both animals start at maximum health.
Each animal attempts one attack per round; attacks are resolved simultaneously.
There is no first-player, queue-order, or CPU attack-order advantage.

For each attack:

```text
hitChance = clamp(0.85 + 0.003 * (attackerSpeed - defenderSpeed), 0.70, 0.95)
hit = hitRoll < hitChance
damageVariation = 0.95 + 0.10 * variationRoll
rawDamage = 2 * attackerPower * damageVariation
damageOnHit = max(1, floor(rawDamage * 100 / (100 + defenderGuard) + 0.5))
damageOnMiss = 0
```

`clamp(value, minimum, maximum)` bounds the value to the stated interval.
Hit and variation rolls are separate pseudorandom values in `[0, 1)`.
Generate both rolls for each animal every round, including misses, using a
server-controlled seed and a versioned pseudorandom generator.

Calculate both attacks from the state at the start of the round, then apply
their damage together. Clamp remaining health to zero. An animal knocked out
that round still lands any successful attack already calculated for that round.

* If exactly one animal reaches zero health, the other wins.
* If both reach zero in the same round, the battle is a draw.
* If neither reaches zero after round 60, higher remaining health percentage wins.
* If those percentages are exactly equal, the battle is a draw.

Compare remaining health percentages using integer cross-multiplication rather
than rounded display percentages. A knockout represents being unable to
continue the virtual match, not death.

### Randomness and reproducibility

Randomness adds uncertainty through misses and a narrow damage range. There are
no critical hits, instant wins, or hidden comeback multipliers.

The server generates the combat seed only after both selections are locked and
all human participants accept the revealed matchup. Store the seed, simulation
version, roster and facility-rules versions, opponent type, revealed conditions,
frozen statistics and bonuses, and ordered event log with the battle.
Weather and terrain are generated and saved
as matchup state before simulation; replay uses the stored conditions rather
than generating them again.

Replaying a stored battle must reproduce the same outcome. Each new battle gets
a new seed. Clients cannot submit rolls, damage, winners, levels, turns, resources,
facility completion times, or XP awards.
Presentation timing never affects the simulated result.

### Example calculation

A level 1 tiger attacking a level 1 lion in neutral conditions, without facility
or readiness bonuses, has an 88% hit chance:
`0.85 + 0.003 * (65 - 55) = 0.88`.
With a variation roll of `0.5`, a successful hit deals 90 damage:
`floor(140 * 100 / 155 + 0.5) = 90`.

The lion starts with 1,150 health. After that hit it has 1,060 health, regardless
of whether its own simultaneous attack hits or misses.

### Battle presentation

Display animal names, levels, health bars, and accessible numeric health values.
Animate attacks, misses, impacts, and knockouts without graphic injury.

Players may independently skip the animation or use reduced-motion presentation.
Skipping reveals the existing server result; it does not cancel the match or
award progression a second time.

The results screen shows the winner or draw, damage dealt, hits and misses,
remaining health, opponent type, XP and resources awarded, and any level gained.
Explain observed differences
such as greater damage or more misses rather than claiming one statistic
definitively caused the entire outcome.

## Progression

### Experience rules

Only the animal used in the completed match earns battle experience. Proposed
base rewards are:

| Outcome | Human battle XP | Human credits | CPU battle XP | CPU credits |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|---------------|-------------|
| Win     | 30              | 30            | 15            | 15          |
| Loss    | 20              | 20            | 10            | 10          |
| Draw    | 25              | 25            | 12            | 12          |

CPU Battle and Quick Battle CPU fallback use the same reduced reward table.
Training-facility XP bonuses apply to battle XP, with the final award rounded
down. Credits are the single earned construction and care currency in the first
release; there is no player trading or resource purchase.

The cost to advance from current level `L` to `L + 1` is `100 + 25 * (L - 1)` XP.
Subtract each level's cost and carry remaining XP forward, allowing multiple
level gains from one award. At level 20, further XP is not accumulated.

Reaching level 20 requires 6,175 XP, or approximately 206 wins or 309 losses,
against humans before bonuses and daily reward limits. Training actions provide
another XP source. There is no XP loss, animal death, stat degradation, or
consumable recovery cost.

### Fairness and reward limits

Higher levels and completed facilities intentionally provide advantages. Because
matchmaking uses animal level only, opponents at the same level can have different
facility and welfare bonuses. Display those differences before acceptance and
allow either human player to decline without reward or combat penalty.

Proposed reward limits apply to XP and credits:

* Each account can earn rewards from up to 30 completed battles per UTC day across
  both human and CPU modes.
* Only the first 10 completed human battles between the same unordered account
  pair per UTC day can earn rewards, across all matchmaking sessions.
* CPU battles do not use account-pair limits or create fake player accounts.
* After the relevant limit, battles remain playable with a no-rewards notice.
* Declined, canceled, or failed battles do not count as completed battles.

Determine reward eligibility at battle creation and reserve eligible daily slots
atomically. Charge counters to that creation day's UTC date, even if resolution
crosses midnight. A completed battle consumes its slots; an irrecoverably failed
battle releases them. Display final eligibility before presentation begins.

Persist results, XP, credits, and reservation settlement atomically. Repeated
requests for the same battle cannot consume another slot or grant extra rewards.
Players may have different reward eligibility in the same human battle.
These limits discourage farming but do not solve multi-account abuse.

## Training facilities and daily management

### Management loop

Players manage a persistent training operation through facility cards with build
and upgrade buttons, not a placement grid. Cards show completed level, effect,
next-level prerequisites, credit and turn costs, and pending completion date.

Win or lose battles to earn credits, allocate a limited daily management budget,
and return after reset to use completed improvements. Battles remain available
with zero management turns and with no remaining battle reward slots.

### Daily turns and reset

New accounts start with 24 management turns and a proposed 200 credits. At each
00:00 UTC reset, add 24 turns to the existing balance, capped at 48. Turns are
account-wide, non-tradable, and cannot be purchased.

Only facility construction, upgrades, training, and welfare consume turns.
Browsing, selecting animals, queueing, fighting, and watching replays do not.
Show both the UTC reset time and the player's local equivalent.

Apply missed resets on the server without requiring daily sign-in. Apply due
construction, welfare decay, turn accrual, and daily training-limit rollover
before processing any new management action or selection lock. Reset processing
must be idempotent; offline accrual never exceeds 48 turns.

### Proposed facilities

An account starts with Training HQ level 1 and Welfare Station level 1. Other
facilities start unbuilt. Each facility has a maximum completed level of 5.

| Facility         | Effect per completed level                          |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Training HQ      | Raises the maximum level of other facilities        |
| Strength Yard    | Adds 2% Power to all owned animals                   |
| Conditioning Pen | Adds 2% Vitality to all owned animals                |
| Defense Grounds  | Adds 2% Guard to all owned animals                   |
| Agility Course   | Adds 2% Speed to all owned animals                   |
| Welfare Station  | Adds 2% battle XP and improves welfare-action gains  |

Other facility levels cannot exceed the completed Training HQ level. Facilities
apply account-wide, not only to one selected animal. Combat bonuses cap at 10%
per statistic before the separate readiness bonus.

Welfare Station improves a care action's welfare gain from 20 to
`20 + 2 * completedStationLevel`. Its battle XP bonus caps at 10% and does not
multiply training-action XP or credits.

Building any non-HQ facility at level 1 unlocks an associated feature: stat
facilities unlock their bonus and a stat-projection panel; Welfare Station
unlocks care and directed training. HQ levels unlock higher facility tiers.
Future animal additions can have explicit HQ or facility prerequisites, but
none of the six starter animals are gated and no additional species are promised
for the first release.

### Construction and upgrades

For a target facility level `T`, proposed costs are:

| Action                         | Credit cost | Turn cost |
|--------------------------------|-------------|-----------|
| Build or upgrade a non-HQ card  | 100 * T * T | 2 * T     |
| Upgrade Training HQ            | 200 * T * T | 4 * T     |

Costs are paid when the action is accepted. A build or upgrade completes at the
next 00:00 UTC reset, not immediately or after an elapsed 24-hour timer.
The existing completed level stays active until then.

Allow at most one pending operation per facility. Different facilities can be
queued concurrently if turns, credits, and completed prerequisites allow.
A pending HQ upgrade does not satisfy a prerequisite for another purchase.
Require construction to target level 1 and upgrades to target the next level
only; a purchase cannot skip tiers or exceed level 5.
Pending purchases cannot be canceled in the proposed first release; show costs
and exact completion time in a confirmation step.

Check prerequisites and debit resources in one server transaction. Reject
insufficient funds, insufficient turns, stale purchases, and duplicate operations
with explicit messages. Request retries must never debit twice.

### Welfare and readiness

Each animal has a welfare score from 0 to 100, starting at 60. Each daily reset
reduces the score by 10, with a minimum of zero, including days spent offline.
An animal at or above 60 receives a 3% readiness bonus to all four statistics;
below 60 it receives no readiness bonus.

A care action targets one animal, costs 1 turn and a proposed 20 credits, and
immediately increases welfare using the completed Welfare Station's gain,
capped at 100. Reject an action when welfare is already full rather than taking
payment for no benefit.

Welfare never modifies the animal's base statistics, earned level, or
level-derived health. Losing readiness removes only its optional bonuses.
Animals never suffer injury, die, or become unavailable through neglect.
All fights begin at full current maximum health. Returning after time away means optional
readiness needs refreshing, not rebuilding lost progression.

Readiness is frozen at selection lock. Care or a reset after lock affects future
matches only. Display welfare, the active readiness bonus, and the next decay
time on each animal's collection card.

### Directed training

A proposed training action costs 2 turns and 30 credits and immediately grants
10 XP to one animal. Allow one training action per animal per UTC day.
Training XP uses normal level costs and carryover, but no battle XP multiplier.

Reject training at level 20 without spending resources. Training cannot change
statistics already frozen in a queued or accepted matchup. It is an optional use
of the management budget, not a requirement to participate in battles.

## Matchmaking and CPU opponents

### Battle modes

Quick Battle searches the available regional/server matchmaking pool for human
opponents. Dedicated CPU Battle creates a CPU matchup without waiting for humans.
Neither mode uses player-created rooms or invite links.

Proposed Quick Battle flow:

1. The server chooses a weather condition and shows it before selection.
2. The player selects and locks an animal; terrain stays hidden.
3. The server freezes the selection and enters the player into a weather-specific
   queue, with the choice hidden from other players.
4. Search by selected animal level only: start within one level, widening to two
   levels after 10 seconds. Use oldest eligible queue entry as the tie-breaker.
5. After 15 seconds without a suitable human match, offer a labeled CPU battle.
   The player may accept it, continue waiting, or cancel.
6. Once an opponent is assigned, reveal both locked choices and randomly generated
   terrain, along with all level, facility, readiness, and condition differences.
7. Each human participant accepts within 60 seconds; then resolve and store the
   battle and award eligible rewards.

Human matches require the same weather condition because both players must see
that weather before choosing. CPU fallback also addresses sparse weather-specific
queues, not just a low total population. Continued waiting keeps the same weather
and selection; no hidden rerolls or widened level range are applied.

Changing an animal or weather requires canceling and beginning a new selection
flow. Declining, canceling, or timing out grants no rewards and clears acceptance.
Other human participants return to selection rather than being silently inserted
into a new match. Use a proposed five-second retry cooldown after a decline or
timeout to discourage repeated condition fishing.

Permit only one active queue entry or unresolved battle per account.
Assignment and CPU-fallback acceptance must be atomic: a race cannot give the
same player both a human and CPU match. Late requests must reference the current
matchmaking revision and cannot accept stale opponents.

### CPU profiles and fairness

Label CPU opponents in selection, reveal, battle, results, and history.
Never disguise them as connected human players or inflate online-player counts.

The proposed baseline CPU chooses uniformly from the six starter species and
uses the human's locked animal level. Choose its animal before terrain is
generated, without inspecting the human species to select a counter.
CPU profiles have no facility or readiness bonuses in the first release.
Their lower rewards and visible lack of bonuses distinguish them from humans;
they may become easier as the player's base develops.

CPUs use the exact same combat, environment, and seed rules as humans. There
are no hidden stat boosts, scripted outcomes, or adjustments during a fight.
The server automatically accepts for the CPU after the human confirms the
revealed matchup.

### Disconnections and results

* Disconnecting while queued removes the entry; reconnecting starts a fresh
  selection flow.
* Disconnecting before battle creation cancels the proposed matchup without
  rewards; another human participant returns to selection.
* A CPU never takes over an already matched human's animal on disconnect.
* After battle creation, simulation and eligible rewards complete even if a
  browser closes.
* Reconnecting retrieves the stored battle and account snapshot without
  restarting simulation or granting duplicate rewards.

Store a battle before announcing it as completed. A persistence failure produces
an explicit retryable error, not a client-calculated result. Retries reuse the
same battle ID and seed. Queue cleanup does not delete completed battle records.

Results offer Battle Again, which starts a fresh Quick Battle or CPU Battle in
the previous mode. Guaranteed rematches, direct friend challenges, chat, ranked
ladders, spectators, tournaments, and same-device play are deferred.

## Screens and user experience

| Screen           | Required content                                                |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Home             | Sign-in, battle modes, turn and credit balances, reset time      |
| Facilities       | Cards, costs, prerequisites, completed and pending levels        |
| Collection       | Profiles, levels, XP, welfare, readiness, care and training       |
| Animal selection | Mode, weather, roster stats, bonuses, selection and lock         |
| Matchmaking      | Search time, cancel, CPU fallback offer, continue-waiting control |
| Matchup reveal   | Opponent type, terrain, bonuses, reward eligibility, acceptance   |
| Battle           | Animation, health, event log, skip and motion controls           |
| Results          | Outcome, XP, credits, levels, Battle Again, return to facilities  |

Show explicit messages for canceled matches, lost connections, failed sign-in,
stale actions, insufficient turns or credits, failed purchases, and unavailable
battle results. Never silently substitute local progress when server progress
cannot be loaded.

All interactive controls must work with keyboard and touch. Do not convey
outcomes or health states through color alone. Include visible focus indicators,
readable text contrast, mute controls, and reduced-motion support. Provide a
textual battle summary without forcing screen readers to announce every animation.

## Art and audio direction

Use stylized low-poly 3D animals and arenas, with readable silhouettes, grounded
proportions, and strong poses. Keep total art spending under $5,000; use
placeholders during the prototype and prioritize assets that can be reused
across all matchups.
Distinguish species through movement and anatomy, not costumes or human weapons.
Avoid chibi proportions, exaggerated cuteness, and photorealistic injuries.

Use varied terrain arenas with a restrained palette so fighters, condition
indicators, and health bars remain legible. Reuse rigs, framing, and camera
behavior across animals to contain production costs. Facility management uses
cards and reusable icons rather than additional 3D building scenes, preserving
the existing art budget.

Audio uses short animal vocalizations, movement sounds, impact cues, and subdued
music. Audio is optional and starts only after browser-permitted user interaction.
No sound is necessary to understand a result.

## Browser delivery and persistence

Use a browser client for interface and replay presentation, plus an authoritative
backend for matchmaking, CPU profiles, simulation, accounts, and base progression.
Use Microsoft Entra External ID with OAuth 2.0/OpenID Connect for player
authentication, targeting an existing external tenant. For local development,
use a simulated identity behind an explicit development-only flag. It must be
impossible to enable in production and must not create production-valid
identities.

Target a small private beta with minimal monthly hosting costs. Azure and a
proposed ceiling of $50 USD per month were selected for review; the budget is
not approved and must be validated against the beta workload. The framework and
renderer remain undecided.

The proposed first release requires accounts for saved progression. Avoid guest
progress until account recovery and migration rules are designed.

Persistent records include:

* Account ID and chosen display name
* Per-account, per-animal level and residual XP
* Per-animal welfare and last directed-training date
* Account credit balance, management turns, and last processed reset
* Completed facility levels and pending purchases with completion timestamps
* Battle ID, human participant IDs or CPU profile ID, and battle mode
* Locked choices, frozen statistics, facility/readiness bonuses, and XP modifiers
* Terrain, weather, condition modifiers, seed, rules versions, and ordered events
* Outcome, XP and credit awards, and daily reward reservations
* Daily account and human-account-pair reward counters
* Idempotent management transaction IDs and spending records

Use temporary queue and proposed-match state for weather pools, connection
status, selection revisions, acceptance timers, and assignment ownership.
CPU profiles are server-controlled simulation inputs, not login identities.
Authorize all queue, battle, and management operations against the signed-in
account; clients cannot operate another account or submit CPU decisions.

Transmit matchmaking state changes through a real-time connection. After
reconnection, load an authoritative snapshot instead of assuming missed events
were received.
The client renders the stored combat event log; it does not need low-latency
action synchronization because there are no inputs during combat.

Do not collect precise location or real names for gameplay. Retain battle history
while an account is active. After a verified account-deletion request, delete
account-linked history, progression, facilities, and economy records within
30 days, subject to legally required retention and backup expiration. Confirm
regional age, privacy, and
consent requirements before public launch.

### Included

* Six fully selectable animals with distinct base profiles
* Quick Battle human matchmaking with a 15-second CPU fallback offer
* Dedicated CPU Battle and clearly labeled CPU opponents
* Automatic, reproducible battles with a results explanation
* Persistent account-based animal levels and experience
* Facility cards with construction, upgrades, and next-reset completion

### Deferred

* Ranked competition, direct friend challenges, and guaranteed rematches

* Additional animals and terrain types

* Cosmetics, monetization, and seasonal rewards

## Validation and acceptance

### Combat and progression checks

* Every valid pair of animals and condition combination resolves within
  60 rounds.
* Fixed seeds and versioned inputs reproduce exact events and outcomes.
* Stored terrain, weather, and modifiers reproduce the same battle on replay.
* Simultaneous lethal attacks produce a draw.
* Remaining-health comparisons use unrounded ratios.
* Hit chances, damage variation, rounding, and minimum damage match the formulas.
* No player-order or CPU advantage emerges from simulation ordering.
* Facility, readiness, and environment bonuses apply in the documented order,
  with the specified caps and frozen selection state.
* XP costs, carryover, cap behavior, and daily limits match the progression rules.
* Human and CPU reward tables, XP multipliers, and rounding match the rules.
* Duplicate submissions never grant duplicate XP, credits, or turn accrual.
* Client-modified levels, bonuses, damage, CPU decisions, and results are rejected.

### Facility and economy checks

* New accounts receive the documented starters, turns, credits, and welfare.
* Turns accrue across missed UTC resets without exceeding 48.
* Construction finishes on the next UTC reset, including offline completion.
* Pending facilities grant no bonuses and satisfy no purchase prerequisites.
* Concurrent purchases cannot overspend, bypass tiers, or charge twice.
* Welfare decay stops at zero and care stops at 100.
* Welfare never reduces base stats, levels, level-derived health, or availability,
  and every animal starts a battle at full current maximum health.
* Training obeys daily animal limits, XP caps, costs, and locked-match snapshots.
* Reset-boundary operations apply due state changes before new actions.
* Battles remain playable with zero turns or exhausted reward allowances.

### Balance targets

Run at least 10,000 seeds for each of the 15 distinct equal-level species
pairings at levels 1, 10, and 20 across every terrain and weather combination.
Count a draw as half a win for each contender. Also test mirror matches and both
participant orderings.

The initial target is a 35% to 65% score rate for either species in every
equal-level pairing, and a 45% to 55% aggregate score rate per species against
the other five when both sides have identical facility levels and readiness.
Treat these as release targets, not existing results.

Test level gaps of 1, 5, and 10 separately. Increasing an animal's level should
not reduce its aggregate score rate against a fixed opponent. Test minimum and
maximum facilities, readiness on/off, and combined environment extremes separately.
Level-only matchmaking does not promise balanced facility development; measure
same-level outcomes at unequal facility investment and player decline rates.
Measure CPU difficulty as player facilities develop. Progression tuning should
create visible advantages without hiding them from players.

If the starter statistics miss the targets, adjust profiles and formula weights
rather than adding undisclosed handicaps. Version every balance change.

### End-to-end acceptance

* Two separate browsers in compatible queues can finish a human match.
* A single player can complete CPU Battle with no other humans online.
* Quick Battle offers CPU fallback after 15 seconds without a suitable human.
* Continuing to wait or canceling does not silently accept a CPU battle.
* Human/CPU assignment races never create duplicate matches.
* Matchmaking uses animal level only and respects the proposed search ranges.
* Another account cannot operate a player's queue, match, or facilities.
* Both players see the same battle ID, outcome, and XP changes.
* Selection privacy, reveal, acceptance, decline, and timeout work as specified.
* Both players see weather before selection; terrain stays hidden until both
  selections are locked and is revealed before acceptance, in both modes.
* Both clients receive identical condition modifiers and battle results.
* Closing a browser after battle creation does not prevent result persistence.
* CPU identity and reduced rewards are visible throughout the battle flow.
* Reconnecting and skipping replay do not restart combat or duplicate rewards.
* Animal and facility progress, turns, credits, and pending upgrades survive
  sign-out, sign-in, and server restart.
* The full gameplay loop works using keyboard alone and at a 360-pixel viewport.
* Supported browsers show a complete non-graphic battle and textual result.

For the prototype, target a playable home screen within five seconds on a
10 Mbps connection with 100 ms latency and a battle presentation of at least
30 frames per second on a mid-range Android phone from roughly 2021. Record the
specific test model before performance acceptance.

## Development milestones

1. Build and test the headless combat simulator and initial balance matrix.
2. Build animal selection, placeholder terrain arenas, replay, and results
   presentation.
3. Add account persistence, human matchmaking, CPU fallback, and acceptance.
4. Add terrain and weather modifiers, then simulate their full balance matrix.
5. Add animal progression, CPU rewards, daily limits, and reconnect protection.
6. Add facility cards, resource spending, daily resets, welfare, and training.
7. Produce low-poly animal art and audio within budget, then validate mobile and
   accessible interaction.
8. Playtest CPU difficulty, human queue times, daily management, progression pace,
   outcome clarity, and balance.

The prototype should answer whether watching the automatic battles is enjoyable
and whether players understand why they won or lost before expanding the roster.
The management prototype should test whether allocating daily turns is engaging
without making welfare upkeep feel like a daily chore.
